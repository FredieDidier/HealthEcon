A coefficient of 0.02 is a two-percentage-point change in the cesarean share per log point of relative fees; the region $[-0.02, 0.02]$ is an economic-equivalence benchmark, not a confidence interval. \citet{gruber1999physician} estimate about four percentage points per US\$1{,}000 of the cesarean-vaginal fee differential, and \citet{grant2009}, using corrected data, about one point. At the delivery-weighted mean cesarean fee of R\$1,963 in the municipality-year regression sample, these map to 3.01 and 0.75 percentage points per log point; the R\$1,939 mean over delivery events of Table~\ref{tab:fee_per_hour} would give 2.97 and 0.74. Neither the confidence intervals nor the conflicting fixed-effect estimates establish price equivalence. The 0.20 to 0.49 log point negative fee gap of the largest states would move the cesarean rate by at most about 0.37 percentage points at Grant's magnitude and 1.5 at the larger one, compared with a for-profit--public gap of 36 points; this is an order-of-magnitude comparison, not an estimate of Brazil's causal price response.
